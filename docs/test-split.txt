JAPANESE-ONLY SPLIT POLICY
==========================

Scope
-----
A course constraint forbids English songs, so this corpus is Japanese-only.
English rows were removed from every tracked and local file by
`lyrics-asr-purge-english`; they are absent, not filtered at read time.

Split unit and grouping
-----------------------
Group by actual vocalist / voice source, never by clip. All clips from one
singer and all clips cut from one recording stay in a single split.
Collaborations connect performers and recordings into one indivisible group,
which can make the realized percentages differ from the target.

Why: test performance should estimate transcription of previously unseen
singers and recordings. A clip-level split can put the same singer, track,
microphone/room condition, or repeated chorus in both train and test and
inflate scores. Validation is for checkpoint and setting choices. Freeze all
choices before using test once. Every baseline and trained system must use
identical test utterance IDs and identical LRCLIB references.

Current Japanese corpus
-----------------------
`data/cleaned/ja_corpus.jsonl` holds 72 songs over 20 speaker groups, split
62/20/18 by song count at seed 42:

  train        44 songs   11 speaker groups
  validation   13 songs    5 speaker groups
  test         15 songs    4 speaker groups

Speaker groups and recording groups are disjoint across all three splits.

Four speaker groups are pinned into test: `aimer`, `king-gnu`,
`satoshi-fujihara`, `vaundy`. These are the speakers of the 9 Japanese songs
whose zero-shot baseline predictions were already computed and cached, so
pinning them preserves that frozen result instead of discarding it. Seed 42
assigns every remaining speaker group to train or validation;
`lyrics-asr-split --pin-test-speakers` implements the same rule generically and
raises an error if a pinned speaker is absent rather than ignoring it.

Corpus selection happens before splitting, and is a rule rather than a
hand-pick. A song enters the pool only if its LRCLIB reference is in Japanese
script (ratio >= 0.60, which removes rows where LRCLIB stored romanized or
translated lyrics), has at least 8 timestamped lines, has a plausible
synced-lyric coverage of the track, and has a non-cover, non-live source video
within 45 s of the LRCLIB duration. Songs per speaker are capped (5 generally,
4 for the pinned test speakers) so no single vocalist dominates and so test
lands inside the requested 15-18 song budget. All 36 exclusions are recorded
with reasons in `data/cleaned/ja_corpus_excluded.jsonl`.

Speaker group assignments are curated and provisional. Producer credits and
mixed vocal tracks need special handling, and Vocaloid recordings use
voicebanks rather than human singers: the requested Inabakumori tracks are
grouped under `kaai-yuki` and `kafu` by voicebank. Review these assignments
against each recording before presenting the corpus as definitive speaker
generalization.

Evaluation unit
---------------
Scoring happens on utterances, not whole songs. Each utterance is a window of
at most ~28 s cut at LRCLIB syncedLyrics line boundaries and shifted by a
per-song scalar offset, so the audio under an utterance is the audio its
reference describes. Audio that no lyric line covers (intros, solos, outros) is
never cut, so the corpus reports both source duration and the smaller used
duration.

Audit and commands
------------------
Report speakers, utterances, source and used hours, and exact train/test
sentence overlap per split; verify speaker/recording disjointness.

```powershell
lyrics-asr-ja-corpus
lyrics-asr-ja-align
lyrics-asr-ja-segment
lyrics-asr-ja-stats
lyrics-asr-validate data/private/ja_utterances.jsonl --require-split
```

Limitations to report rather than hide: 20 speaker groups is a small number of
held-out voices; dialect is `unassessed` for every row, so no dialect-stratified
claim is made; and LRCLIB references may omit performed ad-libs or describe a
different release of the same song.
